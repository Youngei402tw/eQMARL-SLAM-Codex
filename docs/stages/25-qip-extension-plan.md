# Stage 25 — Quantum Information Processing extension plan

## Question

Can a learned public-information allocator decide when and where to consume scarce entanglement more effectively than equally informed learned and heuristic allocators, and does the result persist across resource noise, graph topology, horizon, and budget changes?

This extension does not claim a new Bell violation or a generic quantum-over-classical computational speedup. It studies the sequential resource-management problem created when certified entanglement-assisted requests compete for a limited distribution budget.

## Prospective stages

1. **Stage 25:** register claims, fresh deterministic splits, compute caps, method contracts, and failure gates.
2. **Stage 26:** implement and tune equal-budget DQN, Double DQN, feed-forward PPO, and recurrent PPO allocators; run causal feature ablations on development/validation data only.
3. **Stage 27:** test non-final topology, agent-count, horizon, budget, visibility, and heralded-loss conditions; select one frozen method and lock the confirmatory analysis.
4. **Stage 28:** certify the sealed request family and run the locked paired held-out study without adaptation.
5. **Stage 29:** reproduce all tables from raw records, audit artifacts, and produce a journal-format LaTeX/PDF submission package.

## Information and comparison contract

All trainable allocators observe the same public request gains, active graph, remaining Bell-pair budget, and remaining horizon. They never receive private endpoint inputs or realized future states. They share the same training catalogs, episode count, tuning opportunities, checkpoint rule, and held-out sequences. Dynamic programming and future-clairvoyant solutions are references rather than equal-information learned competitors.

The primary confirmatory comparison is the frozen proposed allocator against the strongest validation-selected learned baseline. Greedy, random, and static allocation are secondary controls. Model-aware and future-aware solutions quantify headroom.

## Fresh split and test lock

Stage 25 generates deterministic development, validation, and test request/catalog manifests under protocol `robust-entanglement-routing-v1`. Test manifest hashes are committed before development. Stage 26 and Stage 27 code must reject any test-labeled catalog or certificate. Stage 28 alone may certify and evaluate the test family after the Stage 27 freeze commit.

## Required additions

- Equal-budget DQN, Double DQN, feed-forward PPO, and recurrent PPO benchmarks.
- Ablations of remaining budget, remaining horizon, and certified gain inputs.
- Three to eight agents on path, ring, star, and sparse random topologies where defined.
- Multiple horizons and Bell-pair budgets.
- Werner-state visibility and heralded entanglement-generation loss conditions.
- In-distribution, unseen-catalog, and unseen-topology evaluation.
- Training decisions, optimizer updates, wall time, parameter count, and inference latency.
- Paired seed-level uncertainty intervals, multiplicity-aware secondary comparisons, violations, failures, and null results.
